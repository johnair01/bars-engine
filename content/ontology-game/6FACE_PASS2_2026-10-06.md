## Anchor

The game finds where a charge sits in the five-element cycle and lets the body finish the turn. Pass one made the
output a map and the WAVE a breathed ladder whose steps can be blocked and returned to. This pass makes the trip
legible while the player is inside it: how big a block is, what exactly it blocked, what the player could
appreciate, where the trip began, and what the six faces are before the player has to pick one.

## Shaman (magenta)

**The Shaman hears one request under the five: do not let the player get lost in the forest.** Wendell calls the
game "a micro trip through the forest of ones interiors". A forest trip has a trailhead, a trail and a way out to the
place you started. The game has the trail (the route log) and the map at the end, but it never marks the trailhead,
and so a player three blocks deep has no way to say what brought them in. The Shaman wants the trailhead marked in
the player's own words, kept visible on every screen, and visited again at the end.

**The Shaman wants the size of a block to be felt before it is counted.** A slider lets the body answer without
finding a word. The Shaman would show a picture word beside the number (a pebble, a stone, a rock, a boulder, a
wall) because a felt sense often arrives as an image. Asking again on the way back shows the player whether the
block got smaller, which is the only proof the detour was worth taking.

**The Shaman wants Appreciate to stay honest.** Wendell's words are "people aren't always able to appreciate what
shows up". A blank box asks the player to find something; an optional box with a plain line saying that nothing is
fine lets the player find something or not. The 9 September lesson holds: an offer is not a requirement.

**The Shaman would introduce the six faces as the six game masters.** The faces are characters in Wendell's work, and
a character can be met where a level has to be learned. The Shaman holds this as a preference and dissents from the
recommended labels below.

## Architect (orange)

**The Architect says the trailhead is one record the game sets once.** It is set on the first body scan of a cycle,
outside any block: the location, the texture, the first channel and face named after it, and an optional line in
the player's words ("What brought you here?"). Block work never overwrites it, because block work runs inside the
stack and the trailhead sits outside it. A new cycle clears it.

**The Architect puts what was blocked into the block's own frame.** Each frame already saves the step and the cycle
state. It now also keeps the charge the player was with (its location, texture and channel when known), the
player's words for what is in the way, and the size before and after. The route log carries the same, so the map
and the list under it can say "Accept was blocked by 'I can't accept he left', size 7, then 3".

**The Architect merges the two bars into one trail.** Pass one put a bar at the top only while a block is open. The
new bar shows whenever a trip is under way. Its first line names the trailhead, and its second line appears inside a
block and names the step it will return to, with the player's words. The bar keeps the id the tests use.

**The Architect closes the loop at Cycle Complete.** Before the map, the game takes the player back to the trailhead
in words: "You started with tightness in your throat (Anger)." It asks the player to look there now and offers three
answers: it has shifted, it is the same, or something else is there now. The answer is logged and the map marks the
trailhead with a flag. This needs no new phase, so the guided demo's keys do not move.

**The faces need no new data, only new display.** The game keys each face by its colour, and the demo clicks
`face-Amber`. The labels can change on screen while every key, the coach code and the saved summary keep the colour
names.

## Challenger (red)

**The Challenger says the first pass graded the wrong return.** Test 1 checked that the machine came back to Accept;
it never checked that a person on the page could say where they came from. Test 3 below checks the page.

**The Challenger names four ways this goes wrong.**

- **A slider that starts in the middle answers for the player.** A default of 5 gets left at 5 and logged as a felt
  size. The slider starts unset, the game records a size only when the player moves it, and the work button does not
  wait for it.
- **The trail bar can turn into clutter.** If it grows with every crumb it stops being read. It has two lines at
  most: where you started, and, inside a block, where you go back to.
- **The player's words become stored text.** The block's words, the Appreciate line and the trailhead line are
  belief-like text. The 24 September boundary covers them the same way: drawn on screen and in the saved image, never
  written to storage. The browser test checks storage for all three.
- **Plain labels can hide the model.** A coach reading a shared code sees colours. If the player saw only plain words,
  the coach and the player would name the same face differently. The colour stays on every card as a tag.

**The Challenger sets a test for the size.** A size given before block work and a size given on return both reach
the route list, and an unmoved slider reaches nothing.

## Diplomat (green)

**Copy for a client who arrived from a link.** The block screen asks "What's in the way? Say it in your own words if
you can", and "How big is it?". The Appreciate box says "What can you appreciate about what showed up? You may not
find anything today, and that's all right." The trailhead line asks "What brought you here today?", and every one of
these is marked optional.

**The Diplomat builds the faces a bridge, not a lesson.** The first time a player picks a face in a session, three
sentences come first: there are six ways of knowing a feeling, none ranks above another here, and the player picks
the one that sounds most like how this feels, or the one the body leans toward. The faces' own questions, which the
game already holds (`precisionQuestions`), go on the cards, because a question invites where a definition teaches.
After the first time the screen shows one short line.

**The Diplomat prefers plain words first.** "Presence", "Power", "Order", "Understanding", "Perspectives" and
"Systems" come from the game's own descriptions and need no primer. The colour sits beside each as a small tag.

## Addendum, 20:16: how each channel does its job

**His words.** "Also a feature of the ontology game is that when a player chooses an emotional Chanel they have to
explain how the emotion is doing its job. Anger- what's is the obstacle or what obstacle is being created Sadness-
what do you care about and how far away is it from you? Fear- what is the threat? And how far is it from you? Joy-
what is the source of aliveness? Neutrality-(I'll actually need help figuring out the job is neutrality in the frame)"

**The reach test answers Neutrality.** The Regent sent the question back to the record and found the answer in his own
book. Mastering the Game of Allyship (compiled 29 May 2026) says: "Each of these emotions has a job. Fear's job is to
detect threat and risk. Anger's job is to identify obstacles to be overcome, or boundaries to be created or
destroyed. Sadness's job is to point you toward what you care about and how far away you are from it. Joy's job is to
show you what's aligned with your delight. Neutrality's job is detachment and perspective, the view that lets you see
the whole." The game's own channel card already says "Ground Being Held". The question therefore does not reach him;
it becomes a position he can flip.

**The Shaman** turns the book's line into two questions a body can answer: "What are you stepping back from?" and
"From there, what can you see of the whole?". **The Architect** puts the answers on the face screen, above the cards,
so the demo's keys do not move, and keeps each answer with the channel it answers. **The Challenger** notes that his
word is "have to", so the face cards wait until every question is answered; the 9 September lesson that an offer is
not a requirement yields to his explicit word here. **The Regent** keeps the answers out of storage, like the belief.
**The Diplomat** shows the book's job line above the questions so a newcomer learns the job while answering it. The
pass was unanimous on this addendum, which is a flag: the record answered it, so nothing was left to disagree about.
