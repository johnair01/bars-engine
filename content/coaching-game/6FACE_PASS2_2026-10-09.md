## Anchor

The anchor is changed by his words today. A visitor walks into a forest with what they are carrying, takes three small
steps that each ask one small act of them, and reaches a clearing at the centre where their own words, Wendell, and the way to
book are waiting. Anyone who already knows what they want can go straight to the centre.

## Shaman (46, Ascent)

**The cast says the growth is underground.** Wood rises inside the earth before anything shows. The walk should do the
same: the visitor's attention grows one step at a time, and nothing at the edge explains what will happen at the
centre.

**The Emotional Body's report gave the walk its shape.** It counted 33 buttons and headings in the old map and found
that the page made a stranger diagnose themselves before they had felt anything. Its walk is a threshold with one line
and one tap, then where it sits in the body, then what it is like, then a clearing where the visitor names the feeling
and hears its job. It cited attention restoration theory for keeping the scene to a few soft elements, and Hick's law
for one act per screen. Both claims hold: the links say what the report says. Its ritual source was cited by title only
and is not used.

**What it would overreach into, and where the Shaman stops it.** The daemon warned it would make the forest so tidy that
the page became something it decided for the visitor. The Shaman keeps "something else", "I can't tell yet" and "Walk
on without saying" on every step, so the visitor can always pass through without answering.

## Architect (26, Reserves)

**The cast says a store is held and the day has not been named.** The site already holds everything the forest needs:
the body places, the textures and the five feelings' jobs from the ontology game, the 3-2-1, Wendell's photo, and the
paragraph about what he does that has been live under his name since 6 October. The build names the day and uses them. Nothing new is invented except the art.

**The Skeptic's research, graded.** It separated measured findings from folklore.
- **Measured:** strangers leave pages in ten to twenty seconds (NN/g), so the first screen must say what this is.
- **Measured:** 57% of viewing time falls above the fold and 74% in the first two screens (NN/g), so the old page lost
  most visitors before its booking section.
- **Measured, attention only:** photos of real people draw attention and stock photos do not (NN/g).
- **Measured, and it cuts the other way:** choice overload has a mean effect near zero across studies (Scheibehenne
  et al. 2010) and appears with complex sets (Chernev et al. 2015). Four prices of one session is a simple set, so the
  research gives no reason to hide or cut the tiers.
- **Guidance:** hidden content works when the way to it is obvious (NN/g on progressive disclosure).
- **Unlinked folklore:** "one call to action always wins", and any evidence that immersive pages or quizzes convert.
  The forest is a bet, and the pass says so.

**The design is one scene.** The page is one full-screen scene with four screens on the way in and the centre at the end. Three rows
of pines part to the sides and a light widens as the visitor walks in. The art is static shapes and CSS, with no images,
video or sound. The old page's tool cards move into a fold at the centre, the crisis line and the credits move to a
footer on every screen, and the old five-station map is retired. Clean Up, Grow Up and Show Up become the "other paths
from here" at the centre, and the 3-2-1 opens there in place.

## Challenger (48, The Well)

**The cast says what they want is there and the reach is broken.** The risk in a forest is a visitor who came to book
and cannot find the rope. The Challenger tested that first: "I know what I need" sits under "Step in" on the first
screen, and `/coaching#book` from anywhere on the site opens the centre with the prices showing.

**The Protector's report named who the forest could lose.** A person in crisis would lose the 988 line, which sat at the
bottom of the old page. A keyboard user lost their place after every click, because focus was never moved. Motion
ignored the reduced-motion setting. The build answers each: 988 is in the footer of every screen and visible on the
first, each new screen moves focus to its heading, all motion runs only under `motion-safe`, and the scroll is instant
when reduced motion is on. The Protector also asked that the "no AI" line stay where the Portland audience can see it,
and it is on the first screen.

**Four tests can prove this wrong.**
1. A visitor reaches all four prices from the first screen in two taps or fewer. If not, the fast lane failed.
2. No price appears on the walk before the centre. If one does, the walk has become a funnel.
3. Wendell walks it on his phone and reaches the centre. If he stops before it, the walk is too long or too strange.
4. With reduced motion on, nothing on the page moves.

## Regent (30, Burning)

**The cast warns against saying the same true sentence until it consumes its subject.** The old page said "book" in
six places. The forest says it once, at the centre, as "Sit down with me".

**Money stays reserved.** The four prices are unchanged and always appear together, with the existing sentence that it
is one session at four prices. The tier links now say that they open Calendly in a new tab, which the Protector found
missing.

**What is preserved.** The crisis note, the credits for Voice Dialogue, Big Mind and Integral Life Practice, the 3-2-1,
the kept sentences, the copy button, and the free doors all remain. The redirect from wendell.masteringallyship.com is
unchanged.

## Diplomat (21, Bite)

**The cast says something is lodged and nothing closes until it is said.** The visitor arrives with something lodged.
The first line says so plainly: "Whatever brought you here came with you."

**The Player's report asked for the centre to be a reflection, not a menu.** The centre opens with "This is what you
carried in" and the visitor's own sentences in a larger serif, then Wendell's photo and the first-person paragraph that has stood under his name on the
old page since 6 October, then one door. The Player asked whether the centre should show the price. The council answers from the record:
cg-money requires all four prices on every booking door, and the Skeptic's research says a door one tap away works when
it is obvious. So the prices sit one tap behind "Sit down with me", all four together.

**Strangers get plain words.** The walk never names the five moves. They stay in the code and in the other paths, so a
visitor who has never heard "Wake Up" never has to learn it to get to the centre.

## Sage (41, Spending)

**The cast says this has a price not yet named.** The price of the forest is that the page now explains almost nothing
before the centre. A visitor who wants to read about the method finds it in a fold at the centre, not on the way in.
That is the trade Wendell asked for ("don't provide to much context").

**Contributions.** The Shaman, through the Emotional Body, gave the four-screen walk and the ways through without
answering. The Architect, through the Skeptic, gave the graded research and the fold for the tools. The Challenger,
through the Protector, gave the fast lane, the crisis footer, focus and reduced motion. The Regent kept the prices and
the credits fixed and cut "book" to one door. The Diplomat, through the Player, gave the centre as a reflection.
Dropped: the Skeptic's two-version test, because the site has no way to count visitors without breaking cg-browser-only,
and the Emotional Body's tracking of where visitors stop, for the same reason.

**The pass is unanimous on the forest, and that is a flag.** The faces agreed because Wendell's steer was specific. The
Challenger's pass-1 dissent on the front door still stands on the board (cg-front-door), and the forest makes it sharper:
a forest at `/coaching` is a larger change to a live booking page than the map was.
