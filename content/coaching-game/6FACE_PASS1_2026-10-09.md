## Scorecard

This is the first pass on the subject, so there are no earlier tests to grade.

## Anchor

A visitor who arrives with something they are stuck on plays a short game that helps them name it, gives them a move
they can use that week, and ends at the service of Wendell's that fits, with every price and every door still in
reach for someone who already knows what they want.

## Shaman (15, Understatement)

**The cast says the height has to be real.** A game in front of a booking page is only worth the extra steps if each
step gives the visitor something. The Shaman holds the lesson of 3 October: the player leaves with a move they can use
in their life. Every station of the game therefore ends in a sentence the visitor keeps, and the booking door is an
offer at the end of that, never the price of it.

**The map should use Wendell's own five moves.** Wake Up, Open Up, Clean Up, Grow Up and Show Up are already his
vocabulary (the daily council, `council/daily.yaml`; the allyship site's check pages). Each of his services already
sits on one of them: the ontology game's W.A.V.E. is a Wake Up, the five feelings and their jobs are Open Up, the 3-2-1
is a Clean Up, the thirty-day challenge is a Grow Up, and the founder track is a Show Up. The visitor plays a station,
and the station shows the door that fits.

**An offer is not a requirement** (lesson of 9 September). A visitor who came to book must be able to skip the game in
one tap.

## Architect (50, The Cauldron)

**The game is a set of stations over one map, held in the page.** Each station is data: its move, one short playable
step, the sentence the visitor keeps, a free door and a paid door. The page renders the five as a map, lights the ones
visited, and composes a record at the end. A station's content comes from material the site already has: the
ontology game's `CHANNEL_JOBS` (the five feelings' jobs and questions), its body-scan textures, the 3-2-1 demo itself,
and the course and founder-track pages.

**The first screen sorts by situation, not by product.** The visitor picks the sentence closest to where they are
("Something keeps stopping me, and I can't see what it is", "A person, or a part of me, has charge on it", and three more), and that
picks the first station. They can walk to any other station from the map.

**Nothing leaves the browser.** The visitor's words live in component state, as in the 3-2-1 demo, and a copy button
is the only way out. This keeps the boundary the ontology game holds (position `oag-record`). The record is written so
it can be pasted into Calendly's notes box when they book.

**wendell.masteringallyship.com should land on the game.** It already points at the site and shows the home page. A
host redirect in `next.config.ts` ships with the same pull request, so no setting changes in Vercel.

## Challenger (25, Candour)

**The exposure is the live booking page.** `/coaching` already takes bookings. Putting a game in front of it can lose a
visitor who wanted to pay and saw a quiz. The Challenger dissents from making the game the whole of `/coaching`, and
proposes the game at `/coaching/play` with a "Play your way in" button on the page as it is. The other faces answer
that the skip link and the booking section stay on `/coaching`, so the risk is a layout question rather than a lost
door. The dissent stands and goes to Wendell as a question.

**Falsification tests, each tied to the decision it unlocks.**
1. A visitor can reach all four Calendly links from the first screen in one tap. If not, the skip link failed and the
   game cannot be the front door.
2. Every station gives a sentence to keep before it shows a paid door. If any station shows a price first, the game is
   a funnel, not a game, and that station is rewritten.
3. Wendell plays it end to end on his phone (his step, below). If he stops before the map, the front door question
   reopens.

## Regent (7, Muster)

**Money is reserved.** The game never chooses a tier for the visitor and never changes a price. Every booking door
shows all four tiers, as the page does now, with the existing sentence that it is one session at four prices.

**What must be preserved.** The crisis note and the 988 line stay on the page and are reachable from the first screen.
The credit line for Voice Dialogue, Big Mind and Integral Life Practice stays. The 3-2-1 stays the Clean Up check's
interface (Wendell, 6 October).

**This is done when four checks hold.** The tests above pass in a browser at phone width, `npm run check` and the build pass, the copy
passes the voice lint, and Wendell has played it.

## Diplomat (35, Sunrise)

**The copy has to welcome a stranger.** The cast warns that this becomes visible fast: the page is public, and the
Portland community is allergic to AI (bars-engine CLAUDE.md). The game says plainly that it runs in the browser with
no account and no AI, as the 3-2-1 demo already does.

**Plain names for the moves.** Each station carries a one-line plain meaning under its move name, so a visitor who has
never heard "Clean Up" knows what it does ("Clean Up: face what has charge on it").

## Sage (9, Restraint)

**The small rule holding this is the one from 9 September: an offer is not a requirement.** It settles most of the
pass. The game is the way most visitors find their door, and the skip link means nobody has to play it.

**Contributions.** The Shaman gave the five moves as the map and the kept sentence at every station. The Architect gave
the station data shape, the in-browser record and the host redirect. The Challenger gave the dissent on the front door
and three tests. The Regent kept money, the crisis line and the credit line fixed. The Diplomat gave the plain
meanings and the no-AI line. Nothing was dropped.

**The pass is not unanimous.** The Challenger would put the game at `/coaching/play` and leave `/coaching` as
it is. The other five would make the game the front door at `/coaching` with the skip link and booking section kept.
