## Anchor

The anchor changes by his words. A visitor walks into the forest and answers the six unpacking questions, one per
screen. The feeling they want becomes their light, they show on a body where the present one sits, and at the centre
they meet their own answers and the offer of Wendell's that fits them best.

## Shaman (37, Household)

**The cast says an arrangement formed without being discussed.** Pass 2 arranged the walk around the body scan and the
loudest feeling, and nobody asked Wendell whether that was the order. His answer is that the feeling question came too
early. The six questions put the wanted experience first, so the visitor meets what they want before what hurts.

**The body carries question 4.** Wendell asked for the body in place of buttons. The visitor touches a figure where
living here sits, and can touch more than one place. The figure is flat SVG, because the ontology game's 3D figure loads
three.js and a model file, which a slow phone pays for (position cf-protections).

## Architect (63, Arrived)

**The cast says something finished starts moving.** The six questions are already finished in the code
(`UNPACKING_QUESTIONS` in `src/lib/quest-grammar/unpacking-constants.ts`), and so are the states
(`src/lib/alchemy/alchemy-graph.ts`) and the jobs (the ontology game's `CHANNEL_JOBS`). The walk reuses all of them, and
`src/lib/coaching/__tests__/unpacking-walk.test.ts` fails if any copy drifts from its source.

**How each question is played.**
1. What experience do you want to create: five options and a box for the visitor's own words.
2. What will that give you: the five satisfied states (triumph, poignance, excitement, bliss, peace), each with what it
   means. More than one can be chosen, and the first becomes the colour of the forest's light, so the visitor walks
   toward it. Later screens show "walking toward" the chosen states.
3. Compared to that, what is life like right now: the visitor's own words, where the problem statement appears.
4. How does it feel to live here: the body, then ten dissatisfied states, two for each feeling. More than one can be chosen.
5. What would have to be true for someone to feel this way: the job of each feeling behind the states chosen, then the
   visitor's answer.
6. What reservations do you have: the six self-sabotaging beliefs, each with how it tends to sound.

## Challenger (49, Molting)

**The cast says the new skin is soft.** Six questions is a longer walk than pass 2's three steps, and every screen is a
place to lose a visitor. The walk answers this two ways. Every question has "Walk on without answering", so nobody is
stuck on one. "I know what I need" still opens the centre from the first screen.

**Tests for this pass.**
1. A visitor can reach the centre from the first screen without answering anything. If not, the walk has become a gate.
2. Typing in any answer box never loses focus. Pass 3 caught this in its own build: a question frame defined inside the
   page re-mounted on each keystroke, and it now lives outside.
3. Wendell walks it again on his phone, and the centre's lead offer matches what he would have told that visitor.

## Regent (59, Dispersal)

**The cast says what was held rigid comes apart.** Pass 2's single door ("Sit down with me") becomes a short list of what
fits, and money stays reserved inside it. The four prices are unchanged and always appear together. Allyship coaching
links to its own page, which says the price comes after a conversation. The books link to their pages; nothing here
sells directly.

**The routing is the council's reading of his words (position cf-routing).** An allyship experience leads with allyship
coaching, then coaching, then *Mastering the Game of Allyship*. Every other experience leads with coaching for
self-sabotage through Emotional Alchemy, because he named Emotional Alchemy and self-sabotage as where most visitors' work gets solved. Choosing joy, as the light or as how
it feels, adds *Igniting Joy*.

## Diplomat (29, Depths)

**The cast says the bottom has another bottom.** The six questions go deeper than a stranger expects from a coaching
page. The copy keeps each question in Wendell's wording and adds one plain line, so a stranger knows how to answer. The
reservations show how each one sounds ("Who am I to want this?") because a stranger recognises the voice before the
name.

**The five experience options are a draft (position cf-experiences).** He asked for "a few options that I know I Can help
with". The council wrote five from his offers: build what you keep putting off, feel alive again, stop repeating a
pattern with people, show up for people treated differently than you, and lead a team or community through something
hard. He can rewrite any of them on the board.
