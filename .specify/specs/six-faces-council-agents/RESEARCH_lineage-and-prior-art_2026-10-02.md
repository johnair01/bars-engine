## 1. The lineage, and why one file says Blue and another says Amber

Clare Graves, a psychology professor at Union College, built the levels from his own data and coded
them as letter pairs, A-N through H-U, alternating between express-self and sacrifice-self systems.
The popular statement is his 1974 article in *The Futurist*, "Human Nature Prepares for a Momentous
Leap", and the leap in the title is the move from the Green system to the Yellow one. Graves never
used the word *tier*. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graves%27s_emergent_cyclical_levels_of_existence,
https://spiraldynamicsintegral.nl/en/1st-vs-2nd-tier/)

Don Beck and Christopher Cowan published *Spiral Dynamics* in 1996, named the levels vMEMEs, and gave
them the colours Beige, Purple, Red, Blue, Orange, Green, Yellow, Turquoise. Ken Wilber worked with
Beck in the early 2000s under those colours, then in *Integral Spirituality* (2006) renamed four of
them to follow the rainbow: Beige to Infrared, Purple to Magenta, Blue to Amber, Yellow to Teal.
(https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Spiral_Dynamics, https://www.integralworld.net/visser101.html)

**So the Blue in `backend/app/agents/__init__.py` and the Amber in `FOUNDATIONS.md` are the same
level, Graves D-Q.** Blue is the 1996 name and Amber is the 2006 name. `.agent/context/game-master-sects.md`
already rules that Amber is canonical in code and documentation. The `__init__.py` docstring is the
one file out of step.

## 2. What each level sees, in the sources' words

**Magenta is** tribal and animistic, "close to the earth and cyclic in outlook", organised around elders,
custom and clan, where people "bond together to endure and find safety" and change travels in ritual
and symbol. Its gift is belonging. (https://www.spiraldynamics.com/book/SDreview_Dinan.htm)

**Red is** "power-driven, assertive, immediate." Beck and Cowan say "primal red rage might be more
effective to surviving a particular situation than green communal peacefulness", which is the
healthy form stated plainly. (https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Spiral_Dynamics)

**Amber is** order, purpose, one right way, and duty to it. Beck and Cowan's own illustration is the Ten
Commandments. Wilber says it is what one activates "in response to chaos".
(https://www.thenextevolution.com/spiral-dynamics/)

**Orange is** autonomy and achievement through rationality and competition. Wilber sees the world as "a
rational and well-oiled machine with natural laws that can be learned, mastered, and manipulated".
Its named unhealthy form is the Mean Orange Meme. (https://paradigm.red/spiral-dynamics-introduction/)

**Green is** "communitarian, human bonding, ecological sensitivity, networking", where "feelings and
caring supersede cold rationality". (https://www.thenextevolution.com/spiral-dynamics/)

**Teal** sees complexity, adapts, and "accepts the existence and necessity of all previous stages".
Graves found that people here processed more in decision tasks because the needs "to be impressive,
to fear judgment, to fit in, or to find absolute truth" had faded.
(https://spiraldynamicsintegral.nl/en/1st-vs-2nd-tier/)

## 3. Neighbouring models, and a name collision the council must not make

Kegan's orders of mind, Loevinger's ego development as scored by the Washington University Sentence
Completion Test, Cook-Greuter's extension to Construct-aware and Unitive, and Torbert's action logics
are routinely correlated with these levels. **Torbert's *Diplomat* is the conformist logic**, the one
that "obeys group norms, and rarely rocks the boat", and sits at Amber. **Wendell's Diplomat is
Green**, and the Torbert logic that corresponds to Green is the Individualist. A council quoting
Torbert must keep the two Diplomats apart. (https://www.mindtools.com/aum48bk/the-seven-transformations-of-leadership/,
https://integralleadershipreview.com/7437-leadership-stage-development-and-its-effect-on-transformational-change/)

## 4. What the model is standing on, honestly

Graves's primary publication was a popular magazine article, his student samples were never
independently replicated, and no validated instrument assigns a person to a level.
(https://www.si-labs.com/en/articles/spiral-dynamics/) The political criticism is that the model is
elitist in practice: Beck credits second-tier "Spiral Wizards" with "superior decisions for all
parties concerned". (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_Dynamics) Cowan himself later said the gap
between tiers "appears to be far narrower than sometimes popularly reported, if it exists at all".
(https://benjamindavidsteele.wordpress.com/2009/08/26/criticisms-of-spiral-dynamics/) Beck and Cowan
split in 1999, Beck and Wilber diverged after 2002, and the "Mean Green Meme" was, in Beck's words,
invented to shame Green; Todorovic's research argues it does not exist.
(https://www.spiral-dynamics.com/documents/MGM_hyp.pdf)

**What this means for the council.** The levels are lenses on a question. They are never a rating of a
person, which is the standing §2c test in the working rules applied to the theory itself.

## 5. Lens, not ladder

Beck and Cowan's own position is that "one vMEME is not better than another" and that the healthy
expression of each is essential to the whole spiral. Wilber's transcend-and-include says each wave
can be reactivated: red drives in an emergency, blue order in chaos, orange achievement for a job,
green bonding with friends. A stage transcended by dissociation rather than integration becomes an
"allergy", and what it carried is lost. (https://www.thenextevolution.com/spiral-dynamics/)

The sharpest critique comes through Jean Gebser, whose integral structure gives the magic, mythic and
mental structures equal standing, where Wilber folds the first two into lower phases "to be passed
through rather than lingered in". (https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Understanding_the_Difference_Between_Ken_Wilber's_Developmental_Stages_and_Jean_Gebser's_Unfolding_Structures_of_Consciousness)

**The warning for the council is exact: if the Sage is the arbiter, the other five become rungs, and
the model collapses back into the ladder it was built to escape.** Pass one's header reads "the Sage
rules". The Regent in the same pass quoted an earlier ruling that "the Sage does not overturn the
Regent". The record holds both, so the question goes to the board.

## 6. Who else runs agents this way

**Nobody found runs seats that are worldviews.** Spiral Dynamics and Integral appear with LLMs as
single coaches, classifiers or one lens: custom GPTs such as "Spiral Dynamics Level Up Coach"
(https://gptstore.ai/gpts/sLhpldKUFZ-spiral-dynamics-level-up-coach), the applied-spiral-dynamics
repos that file "belief pixels" by stage (https://github.com/mettafive/applied-spiral-dynamics), a
Claude Code plugin that uses AQAL as a diagnostic lens
(https://github.com/Integral-Productivity/model-framework-claude-plugin), and a 2026 *Frontiers in
Psychology* paper that scores sentence completions for ego-development stage at 86 percent agreement
with experts (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1853871/full).
One 2026 paper found frontier models reproduce an intended Kegan stage when asked to speak from it
(https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08549), which is the closest evidence that "speak from this altitude"
produces something a reader can recognise.

**The nearest kin are councils whose seats are jobs or hats.** Karpathy's llm-council runs first
opinions, anonymised peer ranking and a chairman synthesis across vendors, with no learning
(https://github.com/karpathy/llm-council). antil697's six-thinking-hats is a Claude Code skill with
five hat agents in parallel and a Blue Hat chair (https://github.com/antil697/six-thinking-hats).
dubs3c/council lets each persona use a different provider (https://github.com/dubs3c/council). Hats
are modes of attention. Altitudes are value systems that can disagree about what matters.

**The lessons list has academic cousins.** Reflexion stores verbal reflections and injects them into
the next attempt (https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366). Agentic Context Engineering (Stanford, 2025)
keeps a playbook with a Reflector that distils lessons and a Curator that merges them without
"context collapse" (https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10012507). Every implementation found keeps one
memory per assistant. One list per face, taught by a human's rulings, was not found.

**The blind-sector finding from pass six is in the literature.** "Beyond the Hivemind" (2026) calls
personas from one model "different masks on the same face" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02618).
"Peacemaker or Troublemaker" finds debate collapses disagreement and recommends mixed agreeableness
across roles (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23055). "Diversity Collapse in Multi-Agent LLM Systems" finds
the collapse comes from interaction structure, with authority-driven groups converging fastest
(https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18005). The 2026 debate survey says role prompts "may not fully substitute
for genuine model heterogeneity" (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26212v1), and one paper reports mixed
teams no less sycophantic than uniform ones (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00914). Anonymising who said
what reduces identity bias (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07517v1).

**What is new here, as far as the search reached:** a council whose seats are worldviews, whose chair
is explicitly second tier, and whose seats are taught by the human's rulings. That is a claim about
what the search surfaced, not proof of absence.

## What this changes

- `faces.yaml` names its colours in Wilber's 2006 scheme and says so; `__init__.py` gets Amber.
- Each face's entry states what it sees that later levels lose, from §5.
- The definition file carries §4 in a sentence, so the council says what it stands on.
- Seats get stated, unequal agreeableness, with the Challenger set to disagree; a second backbone per
  seat is an experiment recorded in the ledger.
- The Sage's authority goes to the board as a question, because the record holds two answers.
