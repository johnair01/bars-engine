**Convened 2026-10-02, evening.** Called by Wendell after he kept all eleven cause names on the board.
His brief, in his words: *"Let's also have a process where we use the 6 faces to come up with new
emergent terms and usage."*

**The record already has a practice for terms, and this pass extends it rather than replacing it.**
Friendcraft's glossary sorts terms into locked, proposed, inherited and contested, says a proposed
term needs his ruling before prose uses it, and says each term must buy a mechanic. *Blind sector*
is the precedent: on 2026-08-22 he wrote that relationships can keep you ignorant of what both of
you benefit from not seeing, a session named the arc, and he locked it the same day.

## Anchor

The council exists to make Wendell's decisions better at the place he makes them, which is an
interface, with chat for context and steering. For terms, that means a word reaches him already
tested, with its usage written down, and he adopts, retires or renames it in one place.

---

## 🌊 Shaman, on felt sense

**A term gives a feeling a handle, and the good ones in the record came out of charge.** *Blind
sector* came from Wendell's sentence about shadow, not from a naming session. *Integral homies*
came from a message where he was glad about a group of friends. The process should listen for
the word that turns up when something matters, before it listens for anything else.

**The standing test applies to words first.** Does the term say something is wrong with a person,
or something about what has been learned? The research found that the "Mean Green Meme" was
coined to shame a level, and the daemon names (*I'm not worthy*, *I don't belong*) are safe only
because a reader recognises the sentence as their own. A term that diagnoses fails.

**The council's own new word has this risk.** *The reach test* came from Wendell's phrase
*constant reaching*. It must stay pointed at the council's questions, as a test the council passes
or fails. It never becomes a word about Wendell's habits.

## 🧠 Architect, on structure

**The states already exist, so the process maps onto them.** A harvested phrase becomes a
*candidate* when it passes the six tests below, *proposed* when Wendell adopts it on the board
(it enters the Proposed section of that repo's glossary), and *locked* only by his explicit ruling.
Two side states sit beside those. *Not yet* means the usage is too thin to judge. *Retired* means a
rule killed it; the glossary keeps it struck through, as it did for WAVE-Spiral, and the harvester
skips it from then on.

**The repo's glossary stays the canon.** Each repo gets a registry, `council/terms.yaml`, that indexes
its terms with their state, source and usage, and points at the glossary row. The registry never
holds a definition the glossary contradicts.

**Two structural tests, both from the record.** The term buys a mechanic, as the friendcraft
glossary demands. It names something no existing term names; WAVE-Spiral was deprecated because the
Five-Move Form already said it in plain words. A third test is new and the harvest proves it is
needed: **collision across repos.** Friendcraft's canon names Show Up's output *the play*.
Flirtcraft's grammar lists *the play* among the deck nouns that belong in a reading and never in an
instruction. Torbert's *Diplomat* sits two levels below this council's.

## ⚔️ Challenger, on what gets wrong

**The harvest found four dead coinages still standing in the record as emphasis.** *Reaching toward*,
*that's a CRM*, *Friendcraft serves the friend*, and *generated invitations are corrupting*. A rule in
the working rules killed each one. Without a retired state, the next session reads them as
vocabulary because they look like vocabulary.

**Three tests, each one a past failure.** Strip the image and say the claim flatly; if nothing holds
it up but the phrasing, it fails (the invitation rule of 2026-08-27). If the term is a noun, look for
the verb under it; *that's a CRM* was a resemblance doing the work of an argument. Ask whether the
term rewards writing into it; *beat* was retired because the prose grew more rhythmic to qualify.

**The council coining words is the risk, so emergence comes first.** A term is used before it is
named. The harvest finds usage in at least two files. A face may still propose a name for a pattern
that recurs with no name, and the working rules already say how: mark it as the council's, in the
sentence, and expect it to be cut.

## 🏛 Regent, on what to preserve

**Whose word it is gets cited every time.** Wendell's word is cited to the message. A source's is
named. The council's is marked as the council's. This is the rule from *Received wisdom*, applied to
vocabulary.

**Inherited terms are never renamed here.** The friendcraft glossary already forbids it, and the
series owns them.

**A lock is his alone.** Adoption to *proposed* is a click on a board row that says what the click
does, which pass two established as conscious approval. Every change of state writes the repo's
decision log, which is what the glossary's header requires. A retired term keeps its struck-through
row with the reason and the date, so an older document stays readable.

## 🎭 Diplomat, on what bridges

**Usage is the half of the request most likely to be dropped, so every term carries it.** Each one
gets a plain definition, a sentence a five-year-old could follow, one sentence using it, and a
*register*: where it may appear. The house voice already draws the line for altitudes, which the
book conceals and the wiki names. A term carries the same kind of line: council only, product copy,
or book prose.

**Two reader tests, both from failures.** Can the reader check the word? *Plumbing test* failed this
on 2026-08-31. Can Wendell act on a sentence that uses it without asking a follow-up? *Reaching
toward* failed this on 2026-08-22.

**Usage settles the collision on *the play*.** Both repos can be right once usage is recorded:
*the play* is a reading word in both, and it never appears inside a move's instruction.

## 📖 Sage, panoramic

**What each face contributed.** The Shaman gave the listening order and the standing test for words.
The Architect gave the states, the registry and the collision test. The Challenger gave the retired
state and three tests drawn from past failures. The Regent gave attribution, the lock, and the
decision-log rule. The Diplomat gave usage, register and the two reader tests.

**The term pass, in five steps.**

1. **Harvest.** `council/harvest_terms.py` finds names already used in two or more files of the
   deliberation record and not in any glossary or registry. A term Wendell coins in a message is
   added by hand with the quotation. A face may add a name for an unnamed pattern, marked as the
   council's.
2. **Six tests.** One per face, written into `council/faces.yaml` as `term_test`.
3. **Usage card.** Plain definition, the five-year-old sentence, one usage sentence, register, whose
   word, what it replaces, collisions.
4. **Board.** Each candidate is a row: adopt, not yet, retire, or a rename typed beside it, with a
   steer box.
5. **Write.** The registry updates; an adopted term enters the glossary's Proposed section; a retired
   one is struck through; the decision log takes an entry for every change.

**The dissent is about coining.** The Challenger would have the council never coin. The Shaman and Architect would let a
face name a recurring unnamed pattern. The record settles it: the working rules permit a claim that
is the session's own if it is marked as such and expected to be cut. No question reaches Wendell.

## Where it lands

On the board, as a Terms section: thirteen rows, each with its usage card and the four actions.
Nothing enters a glossary until he adopts it there, and nothing is locked until he says so.

**Tests for the next term pass.** Every adopted term has its decision-log entry. No retired term
reappears in a new pass or document as vocabulary. The harvest's candidate count is reported as
description, never as a target.
