The Character Sheet

Thirteen lines. Each one is stamped with the chapter that asks for it, so the sheet fills in the order you read.

The book is right that pen and paper hold this. A sheet you can only reach through a screen is a sheet you will not open in the four seconds you need it. Use this to understand the lines — then print one and keep it where you'll find it.
What the good lines have in common: a name in them, or a number, or a sentence somebody could disagree with. I should be more assertive is not a line. To Priya: that I will stop covering is.

Six ways of moving when something needs doing. Your home face is the one you reach for first, before you decide to. It isn't a personality type — it's a habit under pressure.

Every face has a version that shows up when you're depleted. It isn't the opposite of your face. It's your face doing too much of the thing it does, with nothing left to steer it.

The part of you that grabs the controls when something is at stake. It isn't sabotage. It's doing a job for you, and it has been for a long time. Name the job and you can start renegotiating it.

Five channels — fear, sadness, joy, anger, neutrality. Each one is telling you something specific. One of them you convert into a to-do before it finishes the sentence.
